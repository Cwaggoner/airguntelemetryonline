Precision Fundamentals for Airguns

Sub-MOA principles adapted for air rifles and pistols out to 100 yards

These are practical, field-tested concepts drawn from widely known precision shooting principles and scaled to the distances and velocities typical of airguns. They are original explanations for airgun use and are not a substitute for formal training or manufacturer data.

What "Sub-MOA" Means at Airgun Ranges

One minute of angle (MOA) is approximately 1.047 inches at 100 yards. At 50 yards that same angular unit is half the linear size (~0.52"). At 25 yards it is roughly a quarter-inch. For most airguns the practical goal is a consistent group that is a fraction of an MOA at the distances you actually shoot—commonly 25 to 50 yards, occasionally out to 75–100 yards with high-power PCPs.

1 MOA @ 25 yd
~0.26"
1 MOA @ 50 yd
~0.52"
1 MOA @ 75 yd
~0.79"
1 MOA @ 100 yd
~1.05"

A 0.4" five-shot group at 50 yards is already under 1 MOA. At 25 yards the same angular precision is a much smaller physical group.

Position and Hold Consistency

  • Use the same body position every shot. Small changes in cheek weld, shoulder pressure, or hand placement move point of impact more than most shooters expect at airgun ranges.
  • Support the forend consistently. Resting on different points of the stock or using different pressure changes how the gun recoils or settles.
  • Follow through. Keep the sight picture after the shot so you can see where the pellet actually went relative to your hold.
  • For PCPs, manage the fill pressure window. Shooting outside the flat part of the curve introduces velocity changes that open groups.

Zeroing Strategy for 25-100 Yards

Most airgun shooters zero at 25 or 30 yards. This creates a near and far zero with a small trajectory arc in between. Confirm both crossings with the DOPE table in the Ballistics tab rather than assuming a single zero works at every distance.

  • Zero in calm conditions with a stable rest.
  • Fire 5-shot groups, not single shots, before dialing.
  • Record the exact fill pressure and pellet used when you zero.
  • Re-confirm zero after cleaning the barrel or changing pellets.
Practical Airgun Zero Workflow
1. Confirm scope is level (use Level and Cant tab)
2. Shoot a 5-shot group at chosen zero distance
3. Measure offset from point of aim
4. Dial the correction and re-shoot to verify
5. Save the session in the logbook with pellet and pressure

Pellet and Velocity Consistency

Airguns are sensitive to pellet fit and velocity spread. A 10-15 FPS extreme spread that is acceptable in a centerfire rifle can open groups noticeably at 40-50 yards with a light pellet.

  • Test 3-5 pellet brands in the same weight class. Small head-size differences change both velocity and point of impact.
  • Use the Chronograph tab to measure average, extreme spread, and standard deviation before calling a pellet accurate.
  • Stay inside the manufacturer's recommended fill range for PCPs. Velocity drop-off outside that window is a common source of vertical stringing.
  • Keep the barrel clean on a consistent schedule. Leading or residue build-up shifts zero more than most shooters expect.

Environmental Factors at Short Range

Wind still matters at airgun distances, but the effect is smaller than at long range. A 5 mph crosswind can move a light .177 or .22 pellet several tenths of an inch at 40-50 yards. Light and mirage are less of an issue under 100 yards, but temperature swings affect springers and CO2 guns more than regulated PCPs.

Factor Typical Effect (Airgun) What to Do
Crosswind 5 mph 0.2-0.5" at 50 yd (light pellet) Hold into wind or wait for calm
Fill pressure drop Vertical stringing outside sweet spot Stay in flat portion of curve
Temperature swing Velocity change (springer/CO2) Re-chrono and re-zero if large
Scope cant Horizontal error that grows with range Use Level and Cant tab to stay plumb

Practice Methods That Improve Groups

Dry-Fire / Aiming
Practice the same hold and trigger press without pellets. Focus on keeping the reticle still through the break.
5-Shot Groups
Always measure center-to-center, not extreme edge. Use the Shot-Group analyzer to quantify improvement over time.
Distance Ladder
Shoot the same pellet at 20, 30, 40, and 50 yards. Record each group and the required hold or dial.
Log Everything
Pellet, fill pressure, temperature, and group size. Patterns appear only after multiple sessions.

Goal example: a repeating 0.35-0.45" five-shot group at 30 yards with a .22 PCP is strong practical accuracy. At 50 yards the same gun producing 0.6-0.8" groups is already performing at a high level for most airgun platforms.

Common Errors That Open Groups

  • Changing cheek weld or stock contact between shots.
  • Shooting through the end of a PCP fill instead of topping up.
  • Mixing pellet lots or brands without re-zeroing.
  • Ignoring scope cant (even 1-2 degrees shows up at 40+ yards).
  • Judging accuracy from one or two shots instead of consistent groups.
Quick Diagnostic
Vertical stringing → usually velocity variation or inconsistent hold
Horizontal stringing → wind, cant, or inconsistent forend pressure
Group walks over time → barrel fouling or temperature change
One flyer per group → often a bad pellet or trigger jerk
