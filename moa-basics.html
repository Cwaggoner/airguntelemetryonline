What is a Minute of Angle?

A Minute of Angle (MOA) is an angular unit equal to 1/60 of a degree. In shooting, it is commonly used for scope adjustments and holdovers.

At 100 yards, 1 MOA subtends approximately 1.047 inches. For practical field use, many shooters round this to 1 inch at 100 yards.

Common MOA Rules of Thumb

  • 1 MOA ≈ 1 inch at 100 yards
  • 1 MOA ≈ 2 inches at 200 yards
  • 1 MOA ≈ 3 inches at 300 yards (and so on, roughly linear with distance)

Actual subtension is slightly more than 1 inch at 100 yards (1.047"), but the 1-inch approximation is widely used for quick calculations.

Scope Adjustments

Most MOA scopes have 1/4 MOA clicks. One click moves the point of impact roughly 1/4 inch at 100 yards.

Example: A shot impacts 2 inches low at 100 yards. With 1/4 MOA clicks, that is approximately 8 clicks up (2 inches ÷ 0.25 = 8).

This page provides general reference information. Always verify with your specific optic, ammunition, and conditions. Detailed training materials and primary references are recommended for advanced application.
